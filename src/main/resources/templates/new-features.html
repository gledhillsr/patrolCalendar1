Available to everyone

Every patroller sees these, whether or not they hold a director role.

The site now answers at the new domain

Where: any page — just the web address

The same site, the same login, the same data, now reachable at the new domain as well as the old address. Both keep working, so nothing you have linked or bookmarked breaks. It is simply an easier address to give out.

When you arrive on the new address a small welcome line appears at the top of the page, so you can tell which one you came in on.

Members can reset their own password

Where: the login page → If you forgot your password

A member enters their member number or email address and is sent a link that lets them set a new password. The link works once and expires after fifteen minutes.

This is the one to point people at when they write to you locked out — they no longer need you to do anything.

You can now add an attachment to emails

Where: Directors → Email Patrollers, or the e-mail THESE patrollers button on a patroller list

You can attach files to an email to your roster — a refresher schedule, a sign-up sheet, a map. The total of all attachments must be 2 MB or less, and the page tells you the running total as you add them.

The confirmation page reports how many people the message was queued for. Sending happens in the background, and if anything goes wrong you are emailed a summary saying how many got through and which addresses failed.

Download the patroller list to Excel

Where: the Patrollers tab → Download to Excel, or Directors → List Patrollers → the download button

The spreadsheet holds exactly the people the page is showing — the same filters, the same classifications, the same sort order. Narrow the list on screen first and the download follows. On the Patrollers tab that means the trimmed or full roster, whichever you are looking at.

It opens straight into Excel, so it is a good starting point for a roster you need to hand somebody outside the calendar.

Show the full roster, or a trimmed one

Where: Patrollers tab → Show FULL roster / Show Trimmed Roster

The trimmed roster leaves out inactive and “other” patrollers, which is usually what you want. Switch to the full roster when you need everybody. The ? beside the button explains what trimming leaves out, and the Pro and Alumni tick boxes above work either way.

A sample resort to experiment in

Where: the front page of the site → the Sample Resort card

A complete patrol with invented people, for trying anything you are unsure of without touching your own data. Everything in it is reset every night at 2am, so nothing you do there lasts and nothing you break matters.

A good place to practise the shift template screens before doing it for real.

Available to directors

These need a director login. Members never see them.

Import a roster downloaded from nsp.org

Where: Directors → Patroller Maintenance → Import New Patrollers from nsp.org …

Sign in to nsp.org as your patrol representative, export your patrol roster to a .csv file, and hand that file to this page. Every patroller in it who is not already on your roster is added — no typing names in one at a time at the start of a season.

You see what it will do before it does it. The file is read and every row listed: who would be added, with the details being brought across, and everyone else with the reason they are being left out — already on your roster, not a patroller, an ID that is not a number. Nothing is written until you press Import on that page.

Nobody already on your roster is touched. Their ID is what is matched, and an existing record is never overwritten, so anything you have edited by hand survives. Running the same file twice adds nobody twice.

Each new patroller arrives as Basic classification, Full Time commitment and not a director, with their name, email and cell number from the file. Change any of that afterwards on their My Info page.

Each new patroller’s password is set to their last name, so tell them to sign in with their member number and their last name, and change it on the My Info page. It is not case sensitive. Anyone who forgets can use If you forgot your password on the login page, since their email address came across with them.

Enhance Shift Templates to use labels, real start time, and end times

Where: Calendar → click a date → Change Today’s Shift, and Directors → Edit Shift Templates (for Calendar) …

A shift row used to have one Start: box, and most patrols typed a job name into it — “dir”, “mentee”, “swing”. That box is now three: Label, Start Time and End Time.

The Label is what the calendar shows. The Start Time and End Time are the real times. A shift labelled “mentor” running 8:00 to 16:30 still shows simply mentor on the calendar, exactly as before — but underneath, the calendar now knows when it starts and ends.

  • Leave the Label blank and the calendar shows the start time instead.
  • Times can be typed loosely — 8, 8am, 0800, 16, 4:30pm — and are tidied into one consistent form when you leave the box.
  • Type a name into Start Time by mistake and it moves itself to the Label.
  • A shift needs a Label or a Start Time to be saved. A row with neither would be a blank line on the calendar for a shift somebody is meant to work.

Why it matters: this is the groundwork for patrollers syncing their shifts to their own phone calendar. That needs a time a computer can read, which free text never was.

Your own buttons in the page header

Where: Directors → Menu Configuration

Add up to four of your own buttons to the header every member sees — your patrol’s website, a weather page, a volunteer application, a training document. Each button has:

  • Text — what the button says.
  • Url — where it goes. A full web address opens in a new window.
  • Member Only — tick this and the button appears in the blue members bar, visible only to people who have logged in. Leave it unticked and it sits in the grey bar above, for everybody.
  • Enabled — untick to hide a button without losing what you typed.

Clear a button’s text and url to remove it. The four slots are independent, so emptying the middle one does not shuffle the others along.

Assign ‘teams’ of patrollers to any shift template

Where: Directors → Edit Shift Templates (for Calendar) … → choose the template in Use Predefined Shift format → Assign Patrollers to this template

If your patrol schedules by teams, you can now pencil people into a template rather than into one day at a time. Pick a template, press Assign Patrollers to this template, and fill in the slots from the roster list.

Those names are a starting point, not a schedule. They become real when the template is used to build a day — either because the calendar filled an empty day-of-week from it, or because you applied it to a date range. Names already on a day are never overwritten by a template.

Apply <day of week> named template (with assigned patrollers) across a range of dates

Where: Directors → Edit Shift Templates (for Calendar) … → choose the template in Use Predefined Shift format → Apply ‘day of week’ named templates with assigned patrollers to calendar

Put a template onto every matching day between two dates in one go, instead of a day at a time. Choose the from and to dates and press Apply.

The button is greyed out unless both of these are true.

  • The template must be named for a day of the week — Sunday, Monday, Tuesday and so on. That name is what picks the days out of your range: a Monday template lands on every Monday between your two dates and nowhere else. The button is greyed out for any other name, because nothing else has a weekday to match.
  • The template must have patrollers assigned to it. Those patrollers are the whole point — applying is what puts the people onto real days. A day-of-week template with nobody on it does not need applying: the calendar already draws its shifts on every matching day between your Season start date and Season end date (Directors → Web Site Preferences …). So if the button is greyed out on a Monday template, assign the team to it first (Assign Patrollers to this template) and it will come alive.

Any patrollers you have assigned to that template come with it, which is the point: a “Sunday” team is put onto every Sunday in one go. A template with shifts but nobody on them still works — it lays out the day’s shifts and leaves them empty for people to sign up to.

Days that are already scheduled are never quietly rewritten. If any matching day already has shifts, the page stops and lists those dates, and asks whether to skip them or overwrite them. Skip is chosen for you. Overwriting deletes those days’ shifts and the patrollers on them, so it is only offered when the template actually has patrollers to put there. Press Apply again to confirm.

Change a single day’s shift format

Where: Calendar → click a date → Use Predefined Shift format dropdown

Swap one day over to a different set of shifts — a holiday layout, a race day, a night operation. Choose the template from the dropdown and the day is rebuilt from it.

This removes the day’s existing shifts and everybody on them, so if anyone is scheduled you are told how many patrollers will be taken off before anything happens. There is deliberately no way to do this across a date range.

Reorder the shifts on a day

Where: Calendar → click a date → the ▲ and ▼ buttons at the end of each shift row

Move a shift up or down so the day reads in the order you want. The top row only offers a down arrow and the bottom row only an up arrow. Moving a shift takes its assigned patrollers with it.

Directors can see any patroller’s shift history

Where: Directors → choose a name in the roster list → Show Patrollers History…

Shows the shifts that patroller has worked, the same view they see of their own. Useful when somebody asks how many days they have in, or when you are checking whether a commitment has been met.

A Done button brings you back to the Directors page. Only directors can do this; anyone else who tries simply sees their own history.

Bold text and links in a day’s comment

Where: Calendar → click a date → Event Name/Comment

The comment that appears under a date on the calendar accepts a little formatting:

  • <b>bold</b> and <i>italic</i>
  • <br> for a line break
  • <a href=https://example.com>click here</a> for a link, which opens in a new window

Useful for pointing at a sign-up form or a race schedule from the day itself.

A standard format for phone numbers

Where: My Info, and Directors editing a member

Phone numbers now have to be entered as 999-999-9999. They are still optional — only the format changed, and only when somebody edits their details. Numbers already in your roster in another format are left alone until someone next updates them.

Why: a consistent format is what makes text messaging possible, which is on the list below.

Nobody can be put on the same shift twice

Where: the Assign Patrollers grids on a day and on a template

Insert a name that is already on that shift and you are told, rather than finding out when somebody does not turn up. A patroller can of course work the morning and the afternoon — those are different shifts.

A shift that needs more than ten patrollers is entered as several rows sharing the same start and end time. Those count as one shift for this purpose, which is how the person working it thinks of it.

Coming soon

Not built yet, but this is the direction:

  • Sync shifts to a personal calendar (Apple, Google, Outlook). A patroller would see their shifts appear in their own calendar automatically. This is why shifts now need a real start and end time — please fill those in on your templates before your patrollers start signing up, so every day of the season is built correctly from the start.
  • Text-message reminders and urgent notices — the reason phone numbers now have a standard format.
